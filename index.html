About

From research idea to working system

I am drawn to machine learning research that holds up to rigorous experimentation, particularly AI for science, data science and computer vision.

I have taken systems from dataset to deployment: curating data, adapting foundation models, distilling models for speed, validating them with grouped cross-validation and ablations, and wrapping them in tested services and interfaces that people can use.

I have led multidisciplinary teams of up to sixteen members, owning scope, system architecture and delivery while staying hands-on in the code. Away from the model, I enjoy the physical side of engineering: soldering, 3D printing and building robots from first principles.

SEM Batch Identification

A full-stack AI application for battery-electrode microscopy

Built in 48 hours for the Polaron Materials Manufacturing track. The app identifies which manufacturing batch an SEM image of a battery anode came from, explains the call with visible material evidence and states how certain it is. Our team of five placed in the top 8 of 50+ submissions (top 25 of 1,200+ applicants), against teams made up mainly of PhD and master's students, researchers and engineers.

What I built32% of draft claims revised

  • Distilled for speed. Distilled a LightGBM model into a U-Net that segments material phases in 0.3 seconds per image.
  • A second opinion. Combined the segmentation with DINOv2 texture features to cross-check the main batch classifier; a call is marked High confidence only when both agree.
  • Explanations that cite. Analyst, research-librarian, writer and reviewer agents explain each vision prediction and cite retrieved research, using Corrective RAG with the Claude API.
  • Claims checked before output. During testing, the reviewer agent flagged 32% of draft claims for revision before the report was shown.
  • Team result. In leave-one-location-out testing, the app's High-confidence calls were right 11 times out of 12.

Fast & Light

Low-rank adaptation for medical imaging

Adapting a segmentation model to every new anatomy is slow and expensive. With a team of six at the Macao Science Centre's Youth Science and Technology Village, we fine-tuned MedSAM2, a SAM2-based medical foundation model, using LoRA on a single A100 GPU, so the method stays practical for smaller hospitals.

Only the low-rank adapters are trained; the foundation model's weights stay frozen.

MethodChest X-ray

  • Dual-prompt fusion. Automated wide and central bounding boxes are fused as prompts, handling anatomical challenges such as the heart obscuring the lung fields.
  • Preprocessing. Cropping, resizing and CLAHE contrast enhancement standardise X-rays from varied sources.
  • Efficiency. 0.969 Dice in under 72 minutes of training, cutting training time and cost by more than 90% against traditional architectures.
